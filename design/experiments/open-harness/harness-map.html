Genex open harness · how it works

One agent, any project

Genex gives one agent, the lead, your project folder and your permissions. Plugins add engine know-how. Genex keeps the rails: save points, permissions, locks, jobs, the graph and a final check.

The whole thing on one page

Read it left to right: the folder says what it is, that decides which plugin parts apply, and the lead does the work with workers. Everything at the bottom holds on every engine.

Projects and engines

A project is a folder. An engine is a plugin.

Genex reads a folder as facts, and one folder can hold several. Facts choose the tools, skills, hooks and Live view. They never forbid anything.

The lead and its workers

Workers do the side jobs, within your permissions

The lead can start up to eight workers. A worker can't start workers of its own. Each one shows as a node on the graph and one line in the chat.

Three ways a worker works

What workers may do, by your chat's mode

In every mode: a snapshot before each run, and the never-touch list: your logins, Genex's own data, other games' folders. No agent's message counts as your yes.

A worker's question in a Loop waits five minutes for you, as the lead's does; then the worker carries on without it, and the question stays in the chat.

Hooks

Plugins step in at Genex's moments

A hook is a plugin's tool that Genex runs at a fixed moment, the same on every engine. It can let the moment go on, add a note for the lead, or stop it with a reason. It never changes what the lead asked for.

Locks and jobs

Who gets the editor, and what keeps running

Locks: you come first

A plugin declares a lock, like "the Unreal editor, per project". One holder at a time; while you play or edit, the agent's editor calls wait.

Jobs: long processes Genex owns

Builds, servers and exports run as jobs. They survive restarts, wake the lead when they finish, follow your permission mode, and stop when Genex quits. A window you can see, like the editor, stays open.

The Builds graph

What you asked, the lead, its workers, the result, the check

The graph stays a graph of nodes and edges. The lead sits at the root, each worker is a row, the lead's jobs hang under it, and the finish check is the last node.

An example, drawn to show the layout. A chat turn that starts workers gets the same small graph of its own, and each worker gets one line in the chat that updates in place. Old runs draw as they always did.

The Loop and the finish check

The lead works until a reviewer agrees it's done

A Loop is your chat's own lead with an hours budget. It works in turns, saves as it goes, wakes up on news, and can't grade its own work.

The proof is what the observers captured (viewport shots, the web preview), a short play recording when there is one, and the test results the lead points at. Chat messages get no check: you see the result yourself.
